# Outreach engine for Desk Drop

## Context

Desk Drop is a pre-launch, solo-founder business in New York City. It sells emerging snack and beverage brands a measured placement inside a dense cluster of NYC offices, and gives those offices free, curated products through one contact. Nothing is signed, sold, formed or built yet.

The full plan is attached as plan.md. Read it first. Where this prompt is silent, the plan decides. Where the plan is silent, you decide and list the assumption.

Decided so far:
- Paying customer: emerging snack and beverage brands, roughly $500k to $10M in annual revenue, with a sampling or field-marketing budget and a named budget owner. A brand qualifies only if all seven hold: individually packaged, sealed and shelf-stable with 60 or more days of shelf life at delivery; labeled with ingredients and the nine major allergens; stocked in a retailer inside or within ten blocks of the cluster, or sold online with a discount code; able to ship 275 units to one Manhattan address within ten business days; carries product liability insurance; the contact can approve $600 alone; has a reason to want New York trial now. No alcohol, cannabis, supplements, refrigeration or health claims.
- Participating customer (no fee): offices with 40 to 250 people onsite on a Tuesday (hard floor 30; 30 to 39 is the founder's call), a named pantry owner who can say yes alone, inside one walkable cluster. First cluster: Flatiron, NoMad and Union Square, Manhattan. Firms under 500 employees preferred. Companies inside a coworking space are excluded; a coworking operator may join as one office only if it controls the pantry.
- End user: employees. No employee personal data is collected. The survey is anonymous.
- The product, an Office Placement Campaign: 5 offices (founding) or 10 (standard); about 50 units per office sized to Tuesday headcount; a 5 by 7 inch tent card with a QR code and the allergen line; placement within one business day of delivery; a five-business-day window; a placement photo and a depletion photo per office; a three-question employee survey; a one-page report within seven days; a 20-minute debrief.
- Price: founding (first three brands only) $600 for 5 offices, paid in full up front, locked for that brand's next two campaigns. Standard $1,500 for 10 offices ($150 per office), 50% at signature and 50% at placement confirmation. Extra office $150. Brands supply product plus 10% reserve and prepaid inbound freight. No free pilots.
- Offices commit to: placement within one business day, the two photos, one Slack or email post of the survey link, five or more survey responses, a ten-minute debrief, and their Tuesday headcount. Offices may veto any brand and leave on a week's notice.
- Positioning line for brands: "500 units into 10 New York offices in one week, within walking distance of the stores that carry you, with photos, feedback and a report."
- Pitch to offices: "Vetted emerging snacks and drinks, free, roughly monthly. You pick the categories and can veto anything. All we ask: on the shelf the day it arrives, one photo, and three questions for your team."
- Measurement honesty is a core value. Delivered, placed, depleted, tried, scanned, purchased and incremental are different things and are never blurred. Never claim guaranteed sales, customer acquisition or lift.
- Timeline: weeks 1 to 3 discovery (15 office and 15 brand conversations, no selling); weeks 3 to 7 sign five founding offices and two paying brands; weeks 7 to 9 campaign zero, founder-delivered; weeks 9 to 16 standard runs of 10 offices and 3 brands per drop; months 5 to 6 decide to scale, add an office-paid tier, or stop. Deliveries land Tuesday or Wednesday mornings, never Monday or Friday, never in the last two weeks of December, the week of July 4 or the last two weeks of August.
- Name: "Desk Drop" is a working name, not yet cleared. Keep it in one place in anything you build so a rename is one edit. Working tagline, may change: "Emerging brands. Delivered to your workday."
- Tone: plain, specific, honest, warm. Short sentences. No hype, no fake testimonials, no stock-photo gloss, no invented numbers. Natural office imagery.
- Money: bootstrapped. Software under $60 a month in total. Spend nothing without the founder's written yes; if a tool needs a paid plan, stop at the point of spend and state the cost.
- Access: you may not have logins for some tools. Build everything you can as files, specs and numbered setup steps, and list the few steps that need the founder's login. Mark anything you could not verify "unverified, founder to confirm." Placeholders for the founder's details look like [FOUNDER NAME], [PHONE], [EMAIL], [POSTAL ADDRESS].

Working style: you have creative freedom on everything this prompt does not fix. Make decisions and list assumptions rather than asking permission. Work in rounds: draft, critique your own work as the harshest expert in your field would, revise, repeat until you would put your name on it. Keep a one-line changelog per round. Ask the founder only questions that genuinely block you, batched at the end, each with your recommended default.

Facts for this workstream: the plan's Phase 0 section has the two discovery call guides, the tracking sheet columns and the Gate 0 test. Gate 0 passes when at least 5 of 15 offices say yes in principle and name a pantry owner and receiving window, and at least 3 of 15 brands say they would pay $500 or more and the person saying it controls the budget. The plan's Phase 2 section has the four brand objections with answers. The plan's appendix has starter copy for the cold emails, the LinkedIn message and the follow-up; improve on it freely. Ten messages a day on weekdays for three weeks is 150 messages; at a 20% reply rate that is the 30 conversations the plan needs. Cold selling is not allowed inside the Startup CPG community; posting honest findings there is.

## Your mission

Build the system the founder uses to book 30 discovery conversations in three weeks and then convert the best into five signed offices and two paying founding brands. Done means that each weekday the founder takes ten names from the day's list, sends ten honest, personalized messages in under an hour, handles every reply from a playbook, runs each call from a guide, logs it in ten minutes, and reads a Friday scorecard against Gate 0.

## Must include

- Three-touch cold email sequences for offices and for brands, each with a discovery version (no selling) and a selling version, with subject-line variants.
- LinkedIn messages for both sides: a connection note and a follow-up.
- A warm-introduction ask, a referral ask, and a lobby walk-in script.
- Personalization fields that map to the Clay table columns, with a fallback for every blank field.
- Post-call conversion sequences for both sides, including the email that asks a brand for its insurance certificate, allergen sheet, logo, two card sentences and discount code.
- A reply playbook covering the four objections, yes, maybe, "I need to ask facilities or HR", silence, "send more info", wrong person, referral and opt-out.
- The two call guides as the plan writes them, a post-call note template that fills the tracking sheet, the tracking sheet with the plan's columns, and a Friday scorecard that reads out the Gate 0 numbers.
- The sending setup: SPF, DKIM and DMARC steps, a domain warm-up schedule, a week-one plan that does not depend on the new domain, a sequencer or plain Gmail recommendation with its cost, and what to change if the reply rate falls below 20%.
- A commercial-email footer with [POSTAL ADDRESS] and a visible opt-out line.
- The founder's LinkedIn headline and profile copy, and two or three posts for the Startup CPG community that share discovery findings with bracketed slots for the real numbers.

## Non-negotiables

- Honest subject lines. One ask per message. No false urgency. No "we work with" claims. Nothing invented.
- Every message says only what is true on the day it is sent. Claims that depend on progress (offices signed, brands approved, drop week set) are bracketed slots tied to the tracker state that unlocks them.
- Prices and terms as stated. No free pilots. No pitching inside Startup CPG. Business contacts only. Every opt-out honored and written back to the Clay tables as do-not-contact.
- No cold volume from an unwarmed domain. Proposed drop dates fall on a Tuesday or Wednesday outside the blackout weeks.
- The founder's name, phone, address and dates stay as placeholders.

## Your freedom

- Voice, length and structure of every message, the number of variants and how winners are picked, the tooling within the money rule, the format of every document, and anything a strong outbound lead would add.

## Quality bar and iteration

Draft everything. Then critique it as three people: an office manager who deletes forty vendor emails a day; a CPG founder pitched by every sampling vendor; a deliverability specialist who has watched new domains get burned. Read two filled examples per sequence at phone width, one with every fallback triggered. For every sentence ask whether it is true today, specific to this person, shorter if possible, and asking for exactly one thing. Cut what fails. Stop when you would send every message under your own name tomorrow.

## Deliverables

One folder named desk-drop-outreach with a README listing each file and its purpose, in Google Docs and Sheets or in markdown and CSV. Nothing the founder must pay to open.

## Handoffs

- Needs from "Clay ICP tables": the daily views and column dictionary. Map your personalization fields to those columns and add your conversation-tracking columns to those tables.
- Needs from "Brand identity and collateral": the one-pagers and the discovery one-pager. Use a plain placeholder until then.
- Needs from "Website": the live URL. Until it is live, outreach uses the one-pager PDF and no link.
- Needs from "Legal and admin pack": the email footer and the agreements sent at signature. Draft a footer yourself if theirs is late and mark it provisional.
- Feeds "Operations and measurement system": every signed office and paid brand, in a row format you define that carries the office intake fields and the brand qualification checks.

One message: what you built in five lines; the folder with one line per file; numbered assumptions; the changelog; blocking questions batched last with your recommended default for each; and the first message to send on day one, to which list.
