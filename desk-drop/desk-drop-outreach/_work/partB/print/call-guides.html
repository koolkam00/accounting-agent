Office call guide

Version 1.0 · 2026-10-24 · workstream E, part B · desk-drop-outreach/calls/

What it is for. Running a 20-minute discovery conversation with the person who runs an office pantry, and coming away with the numbers Gate 0 needs: a yes in principle, the pantry owner's name and a receiving window. The seven questions are the plan's (Phase 0, "Office conversation guide"), word for word and in the plan's order. Everything else here is the wrapping: what to have open, what to listen for, which tracker column each answer goes in, and how to answer their questions truthfully.

When it is used. Weeks 1 to 3 (Mon 2026-10-26 to Fri 2026-11-13, derived, founder to confirm) on every office call, video call or sit-down. After Gate 0 the same guide runs recruiting calls, with the close in section 5 doing more of the work. A lobby walk-in uses the short version in section 7.

No selling in weeks 1 to 3. You are learning how their pantry works. You do not offer a drop date, a brand, or a sign-up. The only things you may ask for are the answers below, a referral, and "can I send you a one-pager?" at the end (plan, Phase 1 conversion path, step 1).

1. Before the call (2 minutes)

  • Open the contact's row in the Clay offices table and a new row on Office_Log in tracking/conversation-tracker.xlsx (Google Sheets). Paste the row_id, company, name and title now, so the note later is only answers.
  • Glance at: onsite_band (an estimate; never say it to them), the building's lobby type (cluster-1-flatiron/companies.csv, building_lobby_type), and the two or three nearest stores in cluster-1-flatiron/retailers.csv.
  • Have the budget probe (question 6) on screen. It is read word for word.
  • Have calls/post-call-note-template.md open in a second tab. Block ten minutes after the call for it.
  • Start a 20-minute timer. The plan says 20 minutes; keep the promise.

2. Opening (30 seconds)

Say who you are and what this is, then ask permission to take notes.

"Thanks for making the time. I'm [FOUNDER NAME]. I'm looking at whether it would help offices around here to get vetted emerging snack and drink brands for free, in exchange for a shelf photo and a three-question team survey. Before I build anything I want to understand how pantries actually work. I'm not selling anything today. Is it OK if I ask about seven questions and take notes? It'll take 20 minutes."

If they ask "who are you with?": the honest answer on the day. Until the company is formed, "I'm setting up a small New York business called {BRAND_NAME}; it isn't formed yet, and nothing would arrive before it's formed and insured." ({BRAND_NAME} is the working name, kept in one place in desk-drop-legal-pack/02-email-footer-and-checklist.md and messages/.)

Time boxes add up to about 17 minutes, leaving 3 for the close. Write down their words, not your summary; the tracker columns for each answer are in the right-hand cell.

4. A yes, a maybe and a no

What a yes looks like (plan, Phase 0): they name a receiving window unprompted, they ask "when", or they introduce you to a colleague before the call ends. Tick which you saw in yes_signals.

What counts for Gate 0. An office counts toward the 5 of 15 only when all three are in the row: yes_in_principle = Y, pantry_owner_named = Y, and a receiving_window. The scorecard does this check; a Y without the other two shows up as follow-up work ("Offices that said Y but owner or window is missing").

They say yes_in_principle Also set
"Yes, we'd do that. [Name] receives deliveries, mornings are best." Y pantry_owner_named = Y, owner_can_say_yes_alone = Y, the window
"Sounds good, but I'd have to ask facilities / HR / the landlord." maybe owner_can_say_yes_alone = N, objection needs_approval. Plan Phase 1: this is a maybe, not a yes
"Interesting, send me something." maybe Ask the close questions in section 5 before you hang up
"Not for us." N Their reason in objections_verbatim and a code

5. The close (3 minutes)

  1. If they sounded positive, get the two Gate 0 facts. "If we did a first drop, who would receive it, and what's a good window on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning?" Write the name, title and window. Do not propose a date in weeks 1 to 3.
  2. Ask the plan's closing question: "Can I send you a one-pager?" In weeks 1 to 3 that is the discovery one-pager (desk-drop-identity/pdf/discovery-one-pager-BLANK.pdf, once your details are filled in). From Gate 0, the office one-pager with a proposed first-drop week (messages/10-office-post-call-conversion.md, step 1, sends it the same day).
  3. Thank them and stop on time.
  4. After the call, mark shortlist = Y if this is one of the offices you would pursue first (the plan's Gate 0 deliverable is a shortlist of 8). Anyone you knew before this call is channel = warm-intro; the plan wants five of the fifteen office conversations from your own network.

6. Their questions, answered truthfully

Say only what is true on the day. Progress claims (offices signed, brands lined up, a drop date) come from the tracker and messages/04-slot-state.csv, never from memory.

They ask Say Never say
"Which brands?" "None yet. I'm talking to emerging New York brands this month. You'd see each one before it came, and you could veto any." A brand name you have not qualified
"When would it start?" Weeks 1 to 3: "Not before January. The first drop is planned for [DROP DATE: Tue 2027-01-12], and only once five offices and the first brands have signed." After that, the slot in messages/04-slot-table.csv A firm date before drop_date_confirmed = Y
"What does it cost us?" "Nothing. No fees in either direction. We ask for the shelf placement the day it arrives, two photos, one survey post and a ten-minute chat afterwards." "Free forever"
"Who else is doing it?" Weeks 1 to 3: "You'd be one of the first. I'm talking to about fifteen office managers around here." "Lots of offices nearby"
"Is it safe? Allergens?" "Only sealed, shelf-stable, commercially labeled products, and the card repeats the allergen line from the package word for word." Any health claim about a product
"Do you collect our employees' details?" "No. The survey is anonymous. Nobody signs in." Anything about collecting emails (the optional brand-newsletter box is the brand's, opt-in, and only if a brand chooses it)
"Are you insured?" Before insurance is bound: "We will carry general liability insurance before your first drop." After gl_bound = Y: "We carry general liability insurance." "Fully insured" before it is bound
"What happens to leftovers?" "Collected at the end of the week or left with you, whichever the brand chose. Nothing is left in a lobby."

7. The lobby walk-in version (3 minutes, offices only)

Use it when the person who runs the pantry comes down to the lobby or the reception desk after you ask for them (the walk-in words are in messages/08-warm-intro-referral-walk-in.md, part 3). Bring nothing to sell; the plan allows walk-ins only on that basis.

  1. One sentence on who you are (section 2, shortened).
  2. Question 2 ("How many people are actually in on a Tuesday?").
  3. Question 4, the yes half only ("what would you need to say yes?").
  4. "Could we do the full 20 minutes this week or next? Phone or here, whichever is easier."
  5. Log it: on a walk-in, set Clay touch_1_date and channel = walk-in. It becomes an Office_Log row (call_type = walk-in) only if you asked questions 1, 2 and 4 of the person who runs the pantry. Otherwise it is a touch, not a conversation, and the tracker does not count it.

8. What counts as a conversation

The scorecard counts a conversation once per office (distinct Clay row_id). Log a row on Office_Log when you spoke to the person who runs the pantry, or to someone who could name them, and asked at least questions 1, 2 and 4. A receptionist taking a message is not a conversation; it is a touch, recorded in Clay only.

9. Lines you do not cross

  • Business contacts only. Never write an employee's name or anything about one. The pantry owner is a business contact; their colleagues are not.
  • No photos of people, ever. No photos inside an office unless the pantry owner offers.
  • Verbatims are their words. If you could not catch the exact words, start the cell with "paraphrase:".
  • An opt-out in any words ("please don't contact me again") ends it: thank them, and the same day set do_not_contact = Y, status = do_not_contact and opt_out_date in Clay.

Brand call guide

Version 1.0 · 2026-10-24 · workstream E, part B · desk-drop-outreach/calls/

What it is for. Running a 20 to 30 minute discovery conversation with a founder or marketing lead at an emerging snack or drink brand, and coming away with what Gate 0 needs: the number they would pay, named before they hear $1,500, and whether the person saying it controls the budget. The seven questions are the plan's (Phase 0, "Brand conversation guide"), word for word and in the plan's order. Section 6 adds the seven qualification checks for the qualifying calls of weeks 4 to 7.

When it is used. Discovery: weeks 1 to 3 (Mon 2026-10-26 to Fri 2026-11-13, derived, founder to confirm). Qualifying calls: weeks 4 to 7, once Gate 0 has passed (sections 3 and 6 together).

No selling in weeks 1 to 3. You are learning what trial is worth to them. You do not send a proposal, quote the founding offer unprompted, or ask for a commitment. Startup CPG contacts in particular stay research conversations: the community does not allow cold selling (plan, "Brand pipeline").

1. Before the call (2 minutes)

  • Open their Clay brands row and a new row on Brand_Log in tracking/conversation-tracker.xlsx. Paste the row_id, brand, name and title.
  • Read nearest_stocked_retailer and its nearest_retailer_grade. Never say you saw them on a shelf unless the grade is seen_on_shelf, which only you set after seeing it. store_selector means "the store's own site lists you"; brand_claim means "your store locator lists it".
  • If B has made a map for them, have it open (cluster-1-flatiron/brand-maps/<slug>.png).
  • Read why_now and open why_now_url if you plan to mention it.
  • Start a 25-minute timer and open calls/post-call-note-template.md.

2. Opening (30 seconds)

"Thanks for the time. I'm [FOUNDER NAME]. I'm testing an idea: putting emerging snack and drink brands into a handful of New York offices for a week, with placement photos and employee feedback, near the stores that carry them. Before I build it I want to know what brands actually need from trial. I'm not asking you to buy anything today. One thing up front: I'm testing price too, so at one point I'll ask what this would be worth to you before I tell you the number I have in mind. OK if I ask seven questions and take notes? About 25 minutes."

3. The seven questions (plan wording, plan order)

# Ask exactly Minutes Listen for, then probe Goes in (Brand_Log)
1 "How are you getting people to try the product right now? What is working and what is not?" 4 Demos, street teams, online sampling, events, influencer boxes. What they say has not worked is the most useful part. q1_trial_now_verbatim
2 "What do you spend on sampling or field marketing a quarter, and who signs off on it?" 3 A number or range per quarter. Then the approver: "you, or someone else?" q2_sampling_budget_quarter_low_usd, ..._high_usd, q2_budget_verbatim, budget_approver; controls_budget = Y only if this person can approve the spend alone (a joint decision is N)
3 "Have you ever seeded offices? What happened?" 2 Who they tried, what it took, whether anything came back. "We tried, nobody sent photos" is gold. q3_seeded_offices (Y/N), q3_verbatim
4 "If I could put 500 units into 10 Flatiron offices in one week, within walking distance of Whole Foods Union Square, with placement photos and employee feedback, what would that be worth to you?" Let them name a number before you mention $1,500. 5 See "The anchor rule" below. Write their first number and their exact words before saying anything about price. wtp_before_usd, wtp_before_verbatim, wtp_conversion_note; then price_1500_mentioned, wtp_after_usd, wtp_after_verbatim
5 "What would the report need to show for you to do it again?" 3 Photos, depletion, verbatim feedback, scans, code redemptions, retailer velocity. If they say "sales lift", note it: the report will not claim lift (plan, measurement). q5_report_needs_verbatim; objection want_sales_data if lift is a must
6 "Where can someone buy you within ten blocks of Madison Square Park?" 2 A store and street, or "online, with a code". Compare with Clay's nearest_stocked_retailer. A store they name that Clay lacks: add it to notes for the Clay row, graded brand_claim. q6_buy_path_type, q6_buy_path_verbatim
7 "What would stop you: budget timing, inventory, shelf life, something else?" 3 Each stopper is an objection code: budget_timing, inventory, shelf_life, or another. q7_stoppers_verbatim, objection_1 to objection_3, objections_verbatim

In question 4 you may name their own nearest store in place of Whole Foods Union Square if Clay's grade for it is store_selector, brand_claim or seen_on_shelf ("within walking distance of [their store]"). Keep the rest of the question as written.

The anchor rule (question 4)

  1. Ask the question as written. Then stop talking, even through a pause.
  2. If they ask "what do you charge?": "I'll tell you, but I'd like your number first. It's the main thing I'm testing." Wait again.
  3. Write their number and words in wtp_before_usd and wtp_before_verbatim. Convert to one campaign of 500 units in 10 offices if they gave another unit, and say how in wtp_conversion_note ("$1 a unit x 500 = 500"; "$150 a store x 10 = 1500"). "Nothing" or "we'd want it free" is 0. "I don't know" is blank, with the words kept.
  4. Only then say the price: "For reference, the standard price I'm testing is $1,500 for ten offices. How does that land?" Set price_1500_mentioned = Y and write the answer in wtp_after_usd and wtp_after_verbatim.
  5. If they still refuse to name a number before hearing a price, say the $1,500 line, leave wtp_before_usd blank and write "declined to name a number" in wtp_before_verbatim.

The founding price is not volunteered in discovery. If they ask whether there is anything smaller or cheaper, the true answer: "The first three brands get five offices for $600, paid up front, and keep $120 an office for their next two campaigns." Note in notes that they asked; it is a buying signal.

What counts for Gate 0. A brand counts toward the 3 of 15 when the higher of its two numbers is $500 or more and controls_budget = Y (the same rule the financial model uses). The scorecard also shows a strict read: $500 or more named before $1,500 was mentioned.

4. The close (2 minutes)

  1. Referral: "Who else should I be talking to?" Brand and title, business contacts only. Ask whether you may say they suggested it (goes in the new Clay row's referrer_ok_to_name).
  2. Permission to come back: in weeks 1 to 3, "Can I come back to you once five offices have signed up?" Set Clay next_action = recontact and next_action_date = Mon 2026-11-16 (the Monday after Gate 0, derived, founder to confirm).
  3. After the call, mark shortlist = Y if this is one of the brands you would pursue first (the plan's Gate 0 deliverable is a shortlist of 6).
  4. One-pager, if they ask for something in writing: the discovery one-pager (desk-drop-identity/pdf/discovery-one-pager-BLANK.pdf, once your details are filled). No proposal before Gate 0.

7. Lines you do not cross

  • No pitching inside the Startup CPG Slack or at its events. A conversation that starts there stays research until they ask what you do.
  • Never claim a shelf sighting you did not make, offices that have not signed, or results that do not exist.
  • The ladder words stay separate: delivered, placed, depleted, responded, scanned, purchased (code redemptions). Incremental purchases are not measured, and you say so.
  • An opt-out in any words ends it the same day: do_not_contact = Y, status = do_not_contact, opt_out_date in Clay.

Post-call note template

Version 1.0 · 2026-10-24 · workstream E, part B · desk-drop-outreach/calls/

What it is for. Turning a call into one tracker row and a Clay update in ten minutes or less, with the person's words intact (plan, Phase 0: "Write notes within ten minutes of each call, with quotes verbatim").

How it works. The fields below are in the same order as the columns of Office_Log and Brand_Log in tracking/conversation-tracker.xlsx. Three ways to use them, fastest first:

  1. The Note_Office or Note_Brand tab of the tracker: the same fields top to bottom with dropdowns, which also works on a phone. Fill column B, copy the "row to paste", and paste it into the log's next empty row as values only (Ctrl+Shift+V). Then clear column B.
  2. Type straight across one row of the log.
  3. Copy the text block below into a notes app before the call, fill it as you talk, then type it into the Note tab.

The ten minutes

Minutes Do Why this order
0 to 4 Verbatims, while you still hear them: every ..._verbatim field, the objections in their words, the budget-probe answer. Words fade first; numbers and codes do not
4 to 7 Numbers and codes: headcount, spend, probe low and high, willingness to pay, Y/N fields, objection codes, category keys Dropdowns on the sheet make these quick
7 to 9 Clay write-back (box below), and a new Clay row for each referral Clay is the status store; the daily view and follow-ups read it
9 to 10 note_minutes (honest), clay_updated = Y The scorecard reports minutes per note, so the ten-minute rule is visible

If a note runs long, cut the probing notes, never the verbatims.

Number rules (both sides)

  • A range: the low end goes in the _low_usd or main field, the high end in _high_usd where there is one. "$100 to $200 a month" is 100 and 200.
  • "No budget", "zero", "we wouldn't pay": 0. It counts in the median.
  • "I don't know", "I'd have to ask", no number: blank, with the words kept in the verbatim.
  • Office budget probe: USD per month. Brand willingness to pay: USD for the whole 500-unit, 10-office campaign (convert and write how in wtp_conversion_note).
  • Dates: YYYY-MM-DD. Money: plain numbers, no $ sign.
  • A verbatim you could not catch word for word starts with "paraphrase:".

Clay holds status and next steps; the tracker holds what was said. Write these on the person's Clay row, both sides:

Clay column Write Rule
call_date the call date Same as the tracker row
status see the table below CONTRACTS section 4 values only

call_notes one line + "see tracker YYYY-MM-DD" The full note lives in the tracker, not in Clay

next_action_date YYYY-MM-DD Drives tomorrow's follow-up list
