# Desk Drop 0→1 Launch Plan

Oct 23, 2026 · @Andrew Kam

## Verdict

The idea is coherent and worth testing, but the current plan describes a business rather than a way to start one. It has no sequence, no first customer, no budget, no legal footing and a first pilot that is too large to run by hand and too small to prove anything. This rewrite keeps the core and replaces the launch.

**What is sound and stays**

- Two paying customers, one end user. Brands and offices both have to want this, and employees are not a mailing list.
- Brands pay first. Testing one revenue stream before adding four others is right.
- The measurement ladder (delivered, placed, depleted, responded, scanned, purchased, incremental). Most sampling vendors blur these on purpose. Keeping them separate is a selling point.
- Supplement the pantry, do not replace it. Replenishment is a different, heavier business.
- A narrow first brand profile and a dense office cluster.

**What is broken and changes**

1. The plan charges the abundant side and gives the scarce side away without asking anything back. Emerging brands are easy to find by the thousand. Offices with a named pantry owner who will say yes are rare and are the entire asset. Office recruitment is the main job, and offices must commit to something (placement photo, a few survey responses, a two-minute debrief) or they will not place the product.
2. The first pilot is 3 brands × 10 offices × 50 units with zero existing relationships. That is 30 brand-office pairings and 10 delivery stops before you have learned anything. Campaign zero is 1 to 2 brands × 5 offices, founder-delivered, charged from day one.
3. Brands get no path to purchase. An office is not a store. Every brand must have a buy path (a retailer within a few blocks or a direct-to-consumer code), and the office cluster is chosen partly for proximity to retailers that stock the brands. The pitch becomes "trial here, buy there, measured."
4. Desk Drop is written as a courier. Receiving, storing, packing and delivering food in Manhattan without a vehicle will eat the margin and the founder's week. From campaign two, brands ship direct to offices or to one consolidation point, and Desk Drop coordinates, confirms and reports.
5. Pricing is per unit ($3 a unit) which invites comparison to cheaper sampling channels. Price per office placement instead, which maps to what the brand is actually buying: access to a specific room of people.
6. Office willingness to pay is deferred to "later." It costs nothing to ask in the first conversation, so the plan probes it from day one.

The rest of this document is the plan: the revised model, the economics with founder hours, a phased roadmap with gates and kill criteria, scripts, operating procedures, legal and tool checklists and templates.

## The revised model

You sell one thing: a fixed-scope Office Placement Campaign, priced per office, with brand-supplied product, delivered through a roster of committed offices in one walkable cluster.

**Positioning line for brands:** 500 units into 10 New York offices in one week, within walking distance of the stores that carry you, with photos, feedback and a report.

**What brands buy**

| Component | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Offices | 5 (founding) or 10 (standard) in one cluster. Category exclusivity within the drop: no competing product in the same delivery |
| Units | About 50 per office, sized to that office's peak-day headcount (floor 30, ceiling 80). Brand ships 10% extra as reserve |
| Product | One SKU, or two flavors treated as one. Sealed, shelf-stable, commercially labeled with allergens |
| Placement | Within one business day of delivery, in the pantry or kitchen the office names, with a Desk Drop display card carrying the brand story, a QR code and allergen line |
| Window | Five business days, Tuesday to the following Monday |
| Evidence | Placement photo on day one, depletion photo on day three or five, employee survey of three questions with a target of five or more responses per office |
| Report | One page per campaign within seven days: units delivered and placed per office, depletion, response summary, scans and code redemptions, what to do next. Plus a 20-minute debrief |
| Brand supplies | Product, inbound freight to one address, an ingredient and allergen sheet, a certificate of product liability insurance, and a discount code or landing page for the QR |

**Price**

| Tier | Price | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founding (first three brands only) | $600 for 5 offices ($120 per office) | Paid in full before product ships. Brand gives a written testimonial and case-study rights if the campaign executes as promised. Price locked for their next two campaigns |
| Standard | $1,500 for 10 offices ($150 per office) | 50% at signature, 50% at placement confirmation |
| Extra office | $150 each | Same terms |

No free pilots. The question you are testing is whether brands pay. A free pilot answers a different question.

**What offices get and give**

| Offices get | Offices give |
| --- | --- |
| A free drop of one to three vetted emerging brands, roughly monthly as supply allows | A named pantry owner and a receiving window |
| Products matched to a preference profile they set, with a veto on any brand | Placement within one business day of delivery |
| One contact, deliveries on Tuesday or Wednesday mornings, a display card so employees know what it is | One placement photo and one depletion photo |
| Their team's feedback summarized back to them | One Slack or email post of the QR link, and five or more survey responses |
|  | A ten-minute debrief and the office's Tuesday headcount |

Not asked of offices: payment, employee contact details, exclusivity, or storage of anything beyond the drop.

**The budget probe.** Once, at intake, ask: "If this goes well and we offered a guaranteed twice-monthly curated drop of products your team voted for, would that have a budget? Roughly what range per month?" Write the answer down verbatim. Ten answers to this question decide whether Phase 4 is an office-paid tier or more brand campaigns.

**Excluded in the first six months**

- Refrigerated or perishable products, anything needing preparation
- Alcohol, CBD or THC, supplements, anything with health claims
- Brands with no retailer inside the cluster and no online store
- Offices with fewer than 30 people on a Tuesday, more than 250, or outside the cluster
- Coworking spaces, unless the operator itself is the participating customer and controls the pantry

**How a campaign runs**

1. Office intake: preferences, Tuesday headcount, receiving window, veto list. The office joins the roster.
2. Brand qualification: fit, buy path, inventory, insurance certificate. Proposal sent with price.
3. Match: each office on the proposed route approves the brand by reply. Explicit yes required for the first three campaigns; opt-out thereafter.
4. Agreement signed and payment received.
5. Brand ships units plus 10% reserve to the consolidation address by the agreed date.
6. Kitting: display cards, allergen line, QR, one labeled case per office.
7. Drop day, Tuesday or Wednesday morning: deliver, place with the pantry owner, photograph.
8. Day three: photo request, top up from reserve if depleted.
9. Day five: collect leftovers or release them to the office, close the survey.
10. Day seven to ten: report, debrief, renewal proposal into the next cluster or the next month.

**Delivery by phase.** Campaign zero: you carry it. Five offices and roughly 250 units is five to eight cases, one morning with a hand truck and two car rides. Campaigns one and two: you plus a courier or a rented van. Campaign three onward: brands ship direct to each office or to one consolidation point, Desk Drop mails the display kit ahead, and you coordinate and verify. Keep the in-person visit for every new office's first drop. The relationship is the product.

**What has to be true**

1. Three brands per drop, not one. The single largest lever.
2. Six hours or less per brand win, which means a 20% close rate on qualified conversations.
3. 40% of brands renew within 90 days.
4. Offices accept one drop a month without fatigue, and the roster is twice the number of monthly drops.
5. Transport under $40 a stop, or brands ship direct.
6. The $1,500 price holds. At $1,000 the three-brand drop drops to about $47 an hour.

## The 0→1 roadmap

Twenty-four weeks, five phases, four gates. You do not move to the next phase until the gate is met, and each gate has a named failure response so a miss is information, not a crisis.

**Roadmap (transcribed from the embedded diagram: 5 phases, 4 gates, 24 weeks)**

| Phase | Weeks | Work | Gate at the end | Gate test (short form) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | 1 to 3 | 15 office calls, 15 brand calls | Gate 0 | 5 offices say yes; 3 brands would pay |
| Recruit + sell | 3 to 7 | Sign 5 offices, close 2 brands | Gate 1 | 5 offices signed; 2 brands have paid |
| Campaign zero | 7 to 9 | Deliver, measure, report in 7 days | Gate 2 | 80% placed in a day; 1 brand would rebuy |
| Standard runs | 9 to 16 | Two 3-brand drops at $1,500 each | Gate 3 | 1 renewal at $1,500; $50+ per founder hour |
| Decide | 17 to 24 | Scale, add office tier, or stop | (decision rule) | See "After the first standard runs" |

Phases overlap where the work allows: office recruiting and brand selling run in the same weeks because the first brand proposal needs a roster to point at, and the first office pitch needs a brand to name.

**If a gate fails**

- Gate 0, fewer than 3 brands would pay: spend three more weeks testing the office-paid version (would offices pay $100 to $200 a month for guaranteed curated drops?) before deciding. Fewer than 5 offices interested: change the cluster or the office size band, not the model.
- Gate 1, offices sign but no brand pays even $600: the brand-paid model does not work at this scale. Pivot to office-paid or stop. Do not run a free campaign to "build the case."
- Gate 2, placement under 80% within a day or fewer than five responses per office: fix the operating procedure once (usually the display card, the handoff or the Slack post). A second miss means offices are not reliable partners at this price, which is a stop signal.
- Gate 3, no renewal: the report is not worth $1,500 to a brand. Redesign the evidence (add retailer velocity, add code redemption) and run one more drop. A second miss is a stop.

**Time budget.** Phases 0 and 1 need 10 to 15 hours a week, mostly calls and walking. Campaign weeks need 20 or more. If you have less than ten hours a week, stretch the weeks but keep the gates.

**Decision dates.** Put the four gate dates in your calendar now, before the first call. A gate reviewed late is a gate skipped.

## Phase 1: recruit the founding offices (weeks 3 to 5)

Five signed offices inside one cluster, each with a named pantry owner, a receiving window and a Tuesday headcount, before any brand is asked for money. The offices are what you sell. Build them first.

**Targeting rules**

- One cluster. Flatiron, NoMad and Union Square is the working hypothesis: dense mid-size tech, media and professional firms, walkable end to end, and Whole Foods Union Square at the southern edge. If discovery points somewhere else (SoHo, Hudson Square, Dumbo), move, but do not split across two.
- 40 to 250 people onsite on a Tuesday. Below 40, 50 units sit for a week. Above 250, 50 units vanish in an hour and the office wants more than a brand will supply.
- A named owner who can say yes alone. If the answer is "I need to ask facilities and HR," it is a maybe, not a yes.
- Receiving that works: a walk-up lobby or a front desk that accepts packages, or a loading dock with hours. Buildings that require a certificate of insurance for every delivery go on the list for later.
- Headcount mix. Aim for two offices near 50, two near 100, one near 200, so the first report shows a range.

**The pitch, in one breath**

"We bring vetted emerging snack and drink brands to a handful of Flatiron offices, free, once a month or so. You pick the categories, veto anything, and all we ask is that it goes on the shelf the day it arrives, you send one photo, and your team answers three questions. You get the discovery without the vendor emails."

**The founding-office offer**

## Phase 3: run the campaign (weeks 7 to 9)

A campaign is a fixed ten-business-day sequence from product arrival to report. Run it the same way every time, write down every deviation, and the second campaign will take half the hours of the first.

**The campaign calendar** (T = drop day, always a Tuesday or Wednesday)

| Day | What happens | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T minus 10 | Brand ships units plus 10% reserve to the consolidation address | Brand |
| T minus 5 | Receive and count. Check best-by dates (60 days minimum), lot numbers, damage. Photograph the cases. Flag any shortfall to the brand the same day | You |
| T minus 3 | Print display cards, allergen lines, case labels. Send each office its delivery window and the pre-written Slack post | You |
| T minus 1 | Text each pantry owner to confirm. Pack one labeled case per office. Charge phone, load hand truck | You |
| T, 8 to 11 am | Route. Place or hand off, photograph, count, log time | You |
| T plus 2 | Ask each owner for a photo. Estimate depletion. Top up from reserve where empty | You and office |
| T plus 4 | Close the survey. Collect leftovers or release them to the office. Final count and photo | You |
| T plus 7 | Report delivered as PDF | You |
| T plus 10 | Twenty-minute debrief with the brand. Renewal proposal sent the same day | You and brand |

**Per-stop procedure, 15 minutes**

1. Arrive inside the receiving window. Security desk: give the pantry owner's name and your ID. Walk-up: go straight to the owner.
2. Hand the case to the owner, or place it yourself if they said you may.
3. Placement standard: at eye level beside the existing snacks, never inside a cabinet, display card in front, allergen line visible. One Desk Drop display per pantry at a time.
4. Two photos: one wide showing the pantry, one close showing the card and product. Count visible units.
5. Confirm the owner has the Slack post text and will post it today. Thank them. Leave.
6. Log: time, units placed, who received, any issue.

Ten stops is about two and a half hours of stops plus travel. Order the route by receiving windows, walk-up lobbies first.

**The kit per office**

- One case, labeled with office name, brand, unit count, your phone number
- A tent card (5 by 7 inches): brand name, two sentences on the brand, "take one, tell us what you think," the QR code, the allergen line, the Desk Drop mark
- A QR code unique to this office and campaign, so scans are attributable by office
- The Slack post, pre-written, three lines, with the survey link
- Five reserve units kept by you, not left at the office

**Storage.** Sealed cases only, room temperature, clean and dry, off the floor, away from heat. Nothing opened, nothing repacked. See the legal section on whether storing at home triggers the food warehouse license, and ask the state before campaign one.

**When things go wrong**

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Product has not arrived by T minus 3 | Slip the drop exactly one week. Tell every office the same day with the new date |
| Short shipment | Reduce units per office evenly. Tell the brand. Credit the difference on the next campaign |
| Damaged or past-date product | Photograph, remove, do not place. Report to the brand with counts |
| Owner absent on drop day | Deliver to the front desk only if that was agreed in intake. Otherwise return the next morning. Never leave food unattended in a lobby |
| Office declines at the door | Take the case back, log it, redistribute to other offices as reserve. Offices that decline twice leave the roster |
| Complaint or allergic reaction reported | Remove product from that office immediately, notify the brand the same day, document what was said and when. Give no medical advice |
| Brand asks for employee contact details | Decline. The agreement says you do not collect them |

**Campaign zero specifics.** You do all of it. Five stops in one morning. Test the display card on a real shelf with a real product the week before. Keep a running log of hours by activity, because the economics section is only as good as that log.

**Gate 2:** at least 80% of units placed within one business day of delivery, at least five survey responses per office, report delivered within seven days, and at least one brand says on the debrief call that it would buy again.

## Measurement and the brand report

Report what happened, how you know, and what you cannot know. Your measurement ladder is correct; this section attaches a method and a stated limitation to each rung so the report is credible on the first read.

| Measure | How it is captured | Limitation stated in the report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Units delivered | Count at handoff, logged per stop | None |
| Units placed | Placement photo with card visible, count of visible units | The owner may hold some back for later |
| Depletion | Counts from photos or owner reports at day 3 and day 5 | One person may take several; sharing and disposal are invisible |
| Exposure | Office Tuesday headcount, reported as a range per office | Not everyone visits the pantry |
| Survey responses | Three-question form reached by QR and the Slack post | Self-selected, small samples |
| QR scans | Per-office dynamic QR with tracking parameters | A scan is curiosity, not intent |
| Code redemptions | Brand reports redemptions of the campaign code at day 30 | Some would have bought anyway |
| Retailer velocity | Brand pulls store-level sales for the nearest retailer, two weeks before and after, if its distributor or data provider offers it | Confounded by everything else happening that month. Suggestive only |
| Incremental purchases | Not measured in the first campaigns | Say so in writing |

**The employee survey.** Three questions, no login, no email required. Did you try it? Rate it one to five. Would you buy it (yes, maybe, no)? One optional free-text line. Office is captured automatically from the QR. An optional email field with an unchecked consent box for the brand's newsletter, linking to the brand's privacy policy, is acceptable; anything more is not.

**The report, one page per campaign**

1. Header: brand, SKU, cluster, drop date, window
2. Roster table: office by industry and headcount band, units placed, day-5 depletion, survey responses
3. Evidence: two photos per office, placement and day 3 or 5
4. Survey: number of responses, share who tried, average rating, share who would buy, three verbatim comments
5. Engagement: scans by office, code redemptions to date
6. What we learned: three bullets, including anything the brand should change (flavor, size, card copy)
7. Recommendation: renew into which offices, adjust what, or stop
8. Limitations: the standard paragraph, every time

Delivered as a PDF within seven days. The appendix has the layout.

**Your own operating metrics**, tracked per campaign from day one

- Placement lag in hours, per office
- Photo compliance (offices that sent both photos)
- Survey responses per office
- Founder hours by activity
- Hard cost per stop
- One-question satisfaction from the brand and from each office at debrief (would you do this again: yes, maybe, no)

**What "good" looks like for campaign zero.** 80% or more placed within a business day, five or more responses per office, half the units gone by day 5, 60% or more of respondents tried it, and the brand says it would do it again. Sales are not a campaign-zero metric.

## Risks and mitigations

The two risks most likely to end the business are brands declining to pay and founder time consuming the margin. Both are tested by Gate 1 and Gate 3, which is why those gates are cash and hours, not sentiment.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brands will donate product but not pay | High | Phase 0 numbers under $500; requests for a free trial | No free pilots. Founding price paid in full. Gate 1 is cash received |
| Founder time and delivery eat the margin | High | Hours log above 60 per drop; one-brand drops | Three brands per drop minimum from campaign one. Brand-direct shipping from campaign three. Courier quotes |
| Attendance and seasonality thin the audience | High | Monday and Friday placements; late December, July 4 week, late August drops | Tuesday and Wednesday only. Block those weeks in the calendar now. Tuesday headcount in intake |

## After the first standard runs: decision scenarios

At Gate 3 (around week 16) you will be in one of four situations. Decide from the numbers, in writing, on the date already in your calendar.

| Situation | What the numbers show | What to do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Both sides work | 40% or more of brands renew; placement above 80%; contribution above $50 an hour | Scale the brand side. Open a second cluster (SoHo and Hudson Square, or Midtown South). Grow the roster to 20 to 30 offices. Raise the standard price to $1,800 to $2,000 on the strength of two reports. Hire a part-time delivery person at about $25 an hour once you run more than two drops a month. Offer a launch-partner tier: 20 offices, category exclusive, $3,000 to $5,000 |
| B. Brands pay once, offices love it | Renewal under 30%; offices asking when the next drop is | The evidence or the audience is the problem, not the idea. Add code redemptions and retailer velocity to the report, open a second cluster so renewals reach new people, run two more drops. | C. Brands willing, offices unreliable | Placement under 80% twice; photos missing; responses under five | The roster is wrong, not the offer. | D. Neither converts | No renewal, weak placement | Stop. Write a two-page post-mortem with the verbatim objections from both sides. Keep the office relationships warm. Do not drift into a third model without a gate |

**The decision rule, stated once.** Scale if Gate 3 passed and renewal is 40% or more. Switch to office-paid if at least five offices named $100 a month or more in the budget probe and brand renewal is under 30% after two extra drops. Stop if both fail.

**What scaling actually requires.** Thirty or more offices across two clusters, a briefed second person for routes, a standardized kit and report, a web intake form for offices and brands, a price ladder (founding, standard, launch partner) and a renewal calendar. None of it requires outside capital at this size. A $25,000 friends-and-family round would only be worth taking in scenario A to fund the second cluster and the first hire six months earlier.
