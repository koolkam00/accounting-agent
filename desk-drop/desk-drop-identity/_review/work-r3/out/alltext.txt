          An idea I'm testing in Flatiron, NoMad
          and Union Square
          Desk Drop is an idea I'm testing: bring sealed snacks and drinks
          from emerging brands to a handful of offices around Flatiron,
          NoMad and Union Square, about once a month.

          The office would get them free, choose the categories and veto
          anything, and in return put them on the shelf the day they arrive,
          send a photo and share a three-question survey with the team.

          The brand would pay a fixed fee per office and get a one-page
          report that keeps every count separate: units delivered, placed and
          depleted, survey responses, QR scans and code redemptions.

          No personal data about employees would be collected, and the
          survey would be anonymous.

          Nothing is for sale yet; I'd value 20 minutes to hear how your pantry
          or your sampling works today, and whether this would help or get in
          the way.

Marks that must never be mistaken
 Placeholder             [LIKE THIS] with a yellow highlighter, on screen and on paper. Dates derived from a placeholder drop date print as

 SAMPLE                 Red band across the top: "Sample. Illustrative placeholders, not results." Appears whenever any value starts with
                        SAMPLE.

 Mock page              Ink band across the top and a vertical margin label, both "Illustrative placeholders, not results". Used only for the
                        proposal's page 2. It carries the brand's own name, product and dates from the proposal; its text slots are grey examples,
                        never the yellow mark, so a finished proposal has nothing yellow.

 Image slot             Grey dashed box: "Real pantry photo, no identifiable people". A missing photo prints "No photo received" in a white box.
                        Never a stock photo.


Give-way order when a page is full
 Report                 Up to five offices: a pair of photos per office, 0.78 down to 0.5 in. Six to twelve: a strip, one column per office, the
                        placement photo above and the day 3 or 5 photo below, 0.5 down to 0.38 in. Then comments 8.75 to 8.25 pt, tighter
                        spacing, "learned" 9.5 to 8.5 pt; the later row moves to the evidence page (the placement row stays); comments to 8 pt;
                        last resort, every photo moves to the evidence page and page 1 keeps "Photos received: n of N". Never: the numbers, the
                        ladder, a comment's words, the limitations paragraph. Tested: 10 offices with three 70-word comments and a retailer-
                        velocity line, and 12 offices, keep the placement strip. Captions (v1.3): a photo's date prints only when it was recorded
                        ( placement_photo_date , later_photo_date ), never worked out from the drop date; a late office's photo is never
                        called "day 1"; page 2's photos shrink from 1.25 to 0.8 in so it always fits.

 Claims                 One list for every template ( system/dd-template.js ): health claims, structure or function wording ("supports gut
                        health", "boosts immunity"), "guaranteed", "the best", sales lift, ROI, conversions, acquisition, "new customers", "drove
                        sales". A hit is an error: no PDF. Our own negations ("No sales guarantee") pass. Override only with claims_reviewed Y
                        and a written reason.

 Proposal               Thirteen arrangements are tried, each with its own give-way (body 8.8 to 8.5 pt, then tighter section gaps, then the map
                        from its own height down to 0.95 in): the standard columns; the cancellation note under the price; the roster across the
                        full width in two halves; for nine or more offices, three narrow tables with the neighborhoods listed once; the timeline as
                        a full-width strip; the map and stores across the full width (map left; stores one line each, numbered to match the map's
                        pins). Of those that fit, the one with the biggest map wins, then the fewest give-way steps. Up to 12 offices with a
                        landscape map and four stores, tested with the four longest store names in workstream B's file and B's own maps, and
                        with real-length details.

 One-pagers             Office: body 9.5 to 9 pt, card drawing narrower, tighter gaps. Brand: tighter text, smaller ladder notes, tighter gaps,
                        smaller lead and title, lists to 8.5 pt. The contact line moves to its own line when it is long.

 Discovery              Sentences 14 to 12 pt.

 All                    If it still does not fit: a red "does not fit" bar on screen, an error in the panel, and no PDF (exit 2).

When the card is full: the give-way order
The template fits the card itself. If text does not fit, it gives way in this order and tells you on screen and in the render output:
(1) the two sentences shrink from 15 to 12 pt; (2) spacing tightens; (3) the find-it line goes from 11 to 10 pt; (4) a brand logo
shrinks from 0.9 in, to 0.75 in for a wide logo (it is the name in the wide photo) or 0.55 in for a compact one (review round 2);
(5) the QR shrinks from 1.45 to 1.15 in; (6) a two-line brand name goes back to one condensed line if it can; (7) the product
line is left off; (8) the brand name shrinks toward 36 pt, widening again where it can; (9) the two sentences are left off. The
name, the thing the wide photo depends on, gives way after the product line (review round 1).

Long ingredient lists. For a package whose only allergen declaration is its ingredient list, a list too long to share a face with
the story and QR moves to a face of its own, and that face is the front: the brand name (43 to 36 pt), the whole list and "may
contain" line at 14 pt, and where to buy. It is marked "this side faces the room" (9 pt bold) beside the card ID. The other face
carries the story, "Take one" and the QR, and its panel points to the list. If the list is still too long, the "Take one" sentence and
then the buy line leave the front (both are on the other face). If it still does not fit, the card prints a red DO NOT PLACE panel
and the render command writes no PDF. The allergen panel never changes.

Before you print: five checks
 1. The allergen text matches the package word for word. Read it against the package, not against the brand's email.
 2. The on-screen panel says "Ready to print" (no red or yellow items). No yellow highlighter is left on the card.
 3. The QR scans from your phone and opens this office's link.
 4. Chrome print settings: Letter, Scale 100, Background graphics on.
 5. One test print on plain paper before the cardstock run.
