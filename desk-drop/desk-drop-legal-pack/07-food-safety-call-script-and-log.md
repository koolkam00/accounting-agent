## The law, in the state's own words

- The license: "This license applies to any food warehouse facility within New York in which food is held for commercial distribution." Warehouses storing or distributing food other than raw agricultural commodities need an Article 28-D license (NY Department of Agriculture and Markets, Food Business Licensing, https://agriculture.ny.gov/food-business-licensing, opened 2026-10-23).
- The definition: "'Food warehouse' shall mean any food establishment in which food is held for commercial distribution." (Agriculture and Markets Law §500-a(4), nysenate.gov, revision 2026-02-20, opened 2026-10-23). The same section's definition of a retail food store "does not include establishments which handle only pre-packaged, non-potentially hazardous foods". The warehouse definition has no such carve-out. That gap is why we ask.
- The requirement: "No person shall maintain or operate a retail food store, food service establishment or food warehouse" without a license (Agriculture and Markets Law §500-b, opening paragraph, revision 2026-02-20, opened 2026-10-23; re-checked 2026-10-24).
- The fee: "The license fee shall be two hundred fifty dollars provided, however, that food warehouses shall pay a license fee of four hundred dollars." (§500-b(3)). Licenses run "for two years from the date of issuance" (§500-b(2)).

## The exact question

> "Does holding sealed, shelf-stable, commercially labeled snacks and drinks, which are never opened or relabeled, at [my apartment / a self-storage unit] in [BOROUGH] for up to about four weeks, and delivering them free to offices for brands that pay me a fee, need an Article 28-D food warehouse license?"

Say it as written. Then give the facts below, in this order, and stop talking.

## The facts to state

1. I am starting a small business, a one-person LLC in New York City with no employees.
2. Emerging snack and drink brands pay me to put their products in about five to ten offices in Manhattan for one week. The offices get them free. Nobody pays for the food itself, and I sell no food.
3. The products are only sealed, individually packaged, shelf-stable snacks and drinks with the brand's own commercial label, including ingredients and allergens. Nothing refrigerated, nothing perishable, nothing that needs preparation.
4. The brand ships its sealed cases to [my apartment / a storage unit]. The day they arrive I count them and record the best-by dates, lot numbers and any damage. Damaged or out-of-date product is set aside and never delivered.
5. I keep them sealed, at room temperature, clean, dry, off the floor, away from heat and as the label directs, from arrival until the campaign ends: usually two to three weeks, up to about four around the holidays.
6. Before delivery, I take the sealed units, unopened, out of the brand's shipping cartons and put them into one delivery box per office, labeled with the office name. I add a printed card. I never open, change or relabel an individual unit, and nothing is put on the units themselves.
7. On a Tuesday or Wednesday morning I deliver the boxes by hand truck, with a car service or rented van between stops, to the offices, where the office puts them in its pantry for employees to take.
8. A few reserve units stay with me during the week. At the end, leftovers go back to the brand or stay with the office. A unit that has been placed in one office is never moved to another.
9. The amounts: about 550 units for the first campaign, up to about 1,650 later. From about the third campaign, the brands ship straight to the offices, and I hold nothing.

## Follow-up questions (ask the ones that apply)

1. **If yes:** Does a home or a self-storage unit qualify for a license? Is there an inspection before the license is issued? How long does it take? Which form, and where do I send it? The fee in the statute is \$400 for two years: is that right?
2. **If yes:** Would any of these remove the need: (a) brands shipping straight to each office, so I never hold product; (b) holding the cases for one night only; (c) receiving at a licensed warehouse or at one of the offices?
3. **Does step 6**, moving sealed units from the brand's cartons into one box per office, count as repacking for your purposes?
4. **Is any other New York State license or registration needed** for what I described? For example, is it a retail food store if no food is sold?
5. **Is there any New York City agency** I should also ask? (The city's health department regulates some food businesses; we have not checked, and the state may know.)
6. **Can you confirm the answer in writing**, by email? If not, may I have your name, title and a reference number?
