## 2 · The questions, in priority order

Times are a guide to keep the hour on track. **Order:** items 1 to 4, then item 10 (the office page, because no office signs until it is cleared), then items 5 to 9, 11 and 12. Items keep their numbers so the other files' references still hold.

### A. Before you file (about 20 minutes)

**1. The name: keep the working name or rename before filing?** (file 01)
Facts checked: a business already trades as "DeskDrop" (thedeskdrop.com), selling branded chocolate gifts shipped to business recipients' desks, nationwide; it has no USPTO filing the searches found. The USPTO refused an earlier DESK-DROP application for online food ordering and office food delivery as "merely descriptive" under Section 2(e)(1) (serial 99174501, non-final Office action 2025-09-30; abandoned 2026-03-31). The examiner also "found no conflicting marks". All searched 2026-10-23 (file 01, rows 2 to 10 and 20).
Ask: does the existing business create a likelihood-of-confusion risk for office snack sampling in New York? Is a federal application worth \$350 per class (USPTO fee schedule, opened 2026-10-23) for a name the USPTO has called descriptive, or should the business pick a more distinctive name now? If you do not handle trademark questions, who would, and what would a fuller clearance search cost (needs quote)?

**2. The office county in the Articles.** (file 04, sections 1 and 4)
Facts checked: the Articles must state "the county within this state in which the office of the limited liability company is to be located" (LLC Law §203(e)(2)), and publication runs in two papers of "the county in which the office ... is located" (LLC Law §206(a)), both opened 2026-10-23. Working publication figures: \$345 in Albany County, \$1,145 to \$1,745 across the five boroughs (file 04, section 4; vendor prices, not quotes).
Ask: I work from [BOROUGH] and have no office elsewhere. May the Articles name Albany County, and what must exist there (a registered agent, a street address)? If not, I publish in [BOROUGH]'s county.

**3. Signing before the company exists.** (file 03, section 9; file 10, section 13; file 05, Schedule B, item 6)
Facts checked: offices sign in weeks 3 to 5, before formation. The office page has the founder sign for the Company and the office agree now that the Company takes the founder's place once registered; the operating agreement's first written decision adopts every such agreement; each office gets a one-line email. The brand agreement is sent only after formation, with section 13 as a fallback.
Ask: does the office's advance consent, plus the Company's adoption, release me personally under New York law? Does Schedule B do what it says? Is anything else needed (a notice, a countersignature)?

**4. LLC Transparency Act: confirm there is nothing to file.** (file 04, section 2)
Facts checked: the Department of State page states the duty for "non-exempt limited liability companies that were formed under the law of a foreign country", effective January 1, 2026, \$25 per statement (dos.ny.gov, opened 2026-10-23). Holland & Knight (2026-01-13) writes that U.S.-formed LLCs, including New York ones, are exempt. The statute: "Within thirty days of an initial filing of articles of organization ... a reporting company shall file with the department of state a beneficial ownership disclosure", and in the same window "an exempt company shall file ... an attestation of exemption" (LLC Law §1107(d)). A "reporting company" has the federal meaning in 31 U.S.C. §5336(a)(11) "and any regulations promulgated thereunder"; an "exempt company" is one "not otherwise defined as a reporting company that meets a condition for exemption enumerated in 31 U.S.C. § 5336(a)(11)(B)" (LLC Law §1106(b), (c)). Both sections nysenate.gov, revision 2026-09-04, opened 2026-10-24.
Ask: one line: a New York LLC whose only member is a U.S. individual is neither a reporting company nor an exempt company, and so files nothing, not even an attestation of exemption, within the 30 days?

### B. The brand campaign agreement (about 25 minutes, file 10)

**5. Cancellation (section 8).** Before T−10, the brand gets everything back less \$100; on or after T−10, we keep 50% of the price, with the reasons stated. Part 1 prints the T−10 date. If product misses the moved date's T−3 too, either side may treat it as a cancellation on or after T−10.
Ask: will a court read the \$100 and the 50% as a reasonable estimate of loss rather than a penalty? The plan says "ten days"; we used ten business days (T−10, the ship-by date, about two calendar weeks before a Tuesday drop and more across a holiday). Is that reasonable, or should it be ten calendar days? Is the double-slip rule fair to enforce? Is releasing unused product to offices when no return label arrives (section 9) safe?

**6. The liability cap (section 7).** "Our total liability to you for anything to do with this agreement is limited to the fees you paid us for this campaign" (the plan's term).
Ask: is it enforceable as written, or does it need carve-outs (for example gross negligence or wilful misconduct) to hold up? Should it also cover the brand's claims against the offices?

**7. Product indemnity (section 6).** The brand defends us against third-party product claims, except to the extent our mishandling caused the claim; it controls the defense but cannot settle in our name without consent.
Ask: is the mishandling carve-out drawn right? Should the offices also be covered by the brand's indemnity or added as additional insureds on its policy (file 11)? Does anything here conflict with the vendors endorsement we ask for?

**8. The founding statement, case study and price lock (section 3).** The plan's "written testimonial" became "a short written statement of your honest experience", published with the disclosure that the brand was a founding brand at a founding price (FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255, opened 2026-10-23). The lock is \$120 per office, for up to 10 offices (any beyond at \$150), for the next two campaigns, with no end date.
Ask: does the wording meet the Endorsement Guides? Does the FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials also apply (not researched)? Should the lock expire, for example campaigns booked within 12 months after this window?

**9. Non-solicitation, confidentiality and disputes (sections 11, 12, 14).** Six months, offices named for the campaign only, with carve-outs for retail, advertising and an office's own approach. Confidentiality of office identities for two years. New York law, no venue or dispute clause.
Ask: is the scope reasonable and enforceable? Add a venue or small-claims clause?

### C. The office page, privacy notice and footer (about 10 minutes)

**10. Office participation agreement (file 03). Take this right after item 4: no office signs until it is cleared.** It has no money and no remedies against the office (section 7); "we will not hold you responsible for how anyone uses them" (section 5); a reply "I agree" counts as a signature (section 10); and a promise to add the building's owner and managing agent as additional insureds when a building requires it (section 4). The office's employer is named in an [OFFICE LEGAL NAME] slot in the top table and the signature block.
Ask: could an office read any clause as a penalty or a legal conclusion? Is the "I agree" reply a reliable signature? Does the section 4 promise create risk if our carrier cannot name a building (we would then not deliver there)? If the page is cleared, may the review heading come off the signable block today?

**11. Survey privacy notice (file 13).** It says "Anonymous" while the office is known from the link and comments may be quoted.
Ask: is "anonymous" the right word, or "we don't ask who you are"? Should the notice name the form and link providers?

**12. Commercial-email footer (file 02).** In use since week 1. It identifies the email with "This is a business pitch, sent one to one." and offers a reply opt-out (FTC CAN-SPAM guide, opened 2026-10-23).
Ask: is that a clear enough identification as a solicitation?

### D. By email if the hour runs out

**13. Operating agreement (file 05).** The death and incapacity clauses (sections 12.2 and 12.3, routed through LLC Law §701(a)(4)); whether the bank will want anything notarized.

**14. The 120-day publication rule (file 04, section 5).** How to count day 120 when it falls on a weekend, and what a suspended LLC cannot do in practice.

**15. Insurance wording.** The no-employees statement for buildings (file 12). Whether to ask brands later for primary and non-contributory wording or a waiver of subrogation (file 11). Buildings: we will have no contract with any building, only the office page's section 4 promise to the tenant. A review of this pack recalled that New York's Court of Appeals read a blanket endorsement's "written contract with you" as requiring a contract directly between the named insured and the additional insured (Gilbane v. St. Paul, 2018); the decision was not opened (the court and case-law sites refused the request), so this is unverified. Is that right, and should we expect each building to be added by name (CG 20 26)?

**16. Product liability period and claims-made policies.** The brand agreement (section 2(c)) asks the brand to keep us as additional insured from its ship date until 12 months after the window closes, or the latest best-by date if later, because released units stay in pantries and go home with employees. If the brand's policy is claims-made, its retroactive date must be on or before the ship date, with the coverage kept in force or an extended reporting period bought for that time.
Ask: is that period right, and enforceable against a small brand? Is a claims-made policy acceptable on those terms (file 11 check)?
