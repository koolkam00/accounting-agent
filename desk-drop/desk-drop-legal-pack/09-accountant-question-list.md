## 2 · The questions, in priority order

If the hour runs short, stop after question 10 and ask for the rest by email.

### A. Sales tax: does any part of the campaign fee carry New York sales tax?

**1. Is the campaign fee taxable at all?**
Facts checked:
- "Sales of services are generally exempt from New York sales tax unless they are specifically taxable." (TB-ST-175, tax.ny.gov, updated 2026-10-16, opened 2026-10-23)
- Tax Law §1105(c)(1) taxes "The furnishing of information by printed, mimeographed or multigraphed matter or by duplicating written or printed matter in any other manner, including the services of collecting, compiling or analyzing information of any kind or nature and furnishing reports thereof to other persons, but excluding the furnishing of information which is personal or individual in nature and which is not or may not be substantially incorporated in reports furnished to other persons, and excluding the services of advertising or other agents, or other persons acting in a representative capacity, ..." (nysenate.gov, revision 2026-06-05, opened 2026-10-23).
- Our deliverable includes a one-page report built from photos, counts and a survey, written for one brand. Compiling survey answers into a report is close to the "collecting, compiling or analyzing information" the statute names, which is why this is question 1.
Ask: does the report make any part of the fee a taxable information service? Does it matter that each report is for one brand only? Is our fee better described as an advertising or promotional service? Should the invoice show one line or several?

**2. Does holding the brand's product count as taxable storage?**
Facts checked: Tax Law §1105(c)(4) taxes "Storing all tangible personal property not held for sale in the regular course of business". TB-ST-740 (footer updated 2026-09-30, opened 2026-10-23) lists "storing tangible personal property" as taxable. We hold each brand's sealed cases from arrival until the campaign ends (usually two to three weeks, up to about four around the December holidays) as part of the campaign, with no separate storage charge.
Ask: is there any exposure? Should the agreement or invoice say anything about it?

**3. Do I need a Certificate of Authority before the first invoice?**
Facts checked: vendors of taxable property or services must register "at least 20 days before you begin business" (TB-ST-360, updated 2026-10-21, opened 2026-10-23). No fee is mentioned on that page.
Ask: if any part is taxable, when must I register relative to the first invoice (expected late November 2026)? If none is, should I keep a written note of why?

**4. What about things I buy, and the cancellation fees?**
Ask: (a) Do I pay sales tax on printing the display cards and case labels, or does the promotional-materials exemption apply (TB-ST-740 lists "Qualifying promotional materials and related services" as exempt, Tax Law 1115(n), TB-ST-692)? (b) Is the \$100 or 50% kept on a cancellation taxed like the fee it replaces? (c) Is the founding brand's testimonial and case-study permission, given as part of the founding price, a taxable or reportable exchange?

**5. If we ever sell product to offices** (the plan's later, office-paid tier): TB-ST-740 lists "candy and confections", "soda and beer" and "bottled water" as taxable, and "Certain food and drinks" as exempt. Ask: what would change (registration, collecting tax by item, filing frequency)? No action now.

### B. Income tax: what I owe, and when

**6. Estimated payments: how much and when, for 2027?**
Facts checked: individuals "generally have to make estimated tax payments if they expect to owe tax of \$1,000 or more when their return is filed" (irs.gov "Estimated taxes", updated 2026-09-25, opened 2026-10-23).
Ask: with little 2026 income from the business and my [OTHER INCOME: employment / none], what should I pay federal, New York State and New York City for 2027, on which dates, and how do I avoid an underpayment penalty? Can I adjust payments quarter by quarter as campaigns land? If I live outside New York City, which city taxes still apply because the work happens in Manhattan?

**7. New York City Unincorporated Business Tax: where do I stand?**
Facts checked (nyc.gov Department of Finance UBT page, opened 2026-10-23): "A 4% tax rate is charged for taxable income allocated to New York City." "A liability of \$3,400 or less is allowed a credit for the full amount of the tax", with a partial credit from \$3,401 to \$5,400. Form NYC-202 is the return "for Individuals, Single-Member LLCs"; NYC-5UBTI is the individual estimated-tax declaration. The page did not show the gross-income level at which a return must be filed.
Ask: at what net income would I actually owe UBT? At what gross income must I file NYC-202 even if no tax is due? Do I need UBT estimated payments in 2027? Does UBT paid give any credit against my New York City personal income tax?

**8. The Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax: am I below it?**
Facts checked: self-employed people report it when net earnings from self-employment in a zone exceed "\$50,000 for tax years 2025 and prior." and "\$150,000 for tax year 2026 and after." (tax.ny.gov, updated 2026-06-30, opened 2026-10-23). The rates were not on that page.
Ask: confirm I am below the threshold for 2027, and what the rate would be if the business grows past it.

**9. Self-employment tax and the business structure.**
Ask: roughly what self-employment tax do I pay on the business profit? At what profit level, if any, would an S-corporation election make sense? (Not in year one unless you say so.)

**10. The \$25 New York LLC fee.**
Facts checked: an LLC "that is a disregarded entity for federal income tax purposes that has income, gain, loss, or deduction from New York State sources" files Form IT-204-LL; "the filing fee is \$25"; due "on or before the 15th day of the third month following the close of your calendar or fiscal tax year", with no extension (Form IT-204-LL instructions, tax.ny.gov, opened 2026-10-23).
Ask: if the LLC is formed in November 2026 and has only start-up expenses that year, do I owe the \$25 for 2026 by Monday 2027-03-15?

### C. Setting up the books

**11. Start-up and formation costs.** Ask: how much of the filing fees, publication, attorney hour and other costs before the first campaign can I deduct in year one, and how should they be recorded?

**12. Cash or accrual, and deposits.** Standard brands pay 50% at signature and 50% at placement, and a campaign can straddle year end. Ask: on the cash method, is a deposit received in December 2026 for a January 2027 campaign income in 2026? How do I record a refund after a cancellation?

**13. Records and deductions.** Ask: what records do you need for car services, couriers, a storage unit or storage at home, the hand truck and printing? Does home storage support any home-office deduction? I also log my hours by activity for the business model; are they useful to you?

**14. Wave setup.** Ask: which categories should I set up in Wave from day one so year-end filing is simple? Should brand payments by card (processing fees about 3%, plan estimate) be recorded gross or net?

**15. Paying other people.** Ask: if I pay a courier company or a backup person for one route, what forms do I collect (W-9) and file (1099-NEC), and from what amount? If a brand pays through a card processor, will I get a 1099-K? (The plan also requires workers' compensation the day anyone is hired; the attorney covers that side.)

**16. Banking and money flow.** Ask: should I keep a separate tax savings account and set aside a fixed share of each payment? What share do you suggest for the first year?

### D. Before you leave the meeting

**17. One calendar.** Ask the accountant to confirm, or correct, every date for the first 18 months: federal, state and city estimates; the 2026 and 2027 returns; IT-204-LL; any NYC-202; any sales tax filing. Write them below.

**18. Fee and follow-up.** Ask: what would year-end preparation cost, and what do you need from me each quarter?
