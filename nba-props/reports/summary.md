# Does an over predict an under? No.

No. After a player goes over his line, the under hits 51.0% to 52.8% of the time next game for points, rebounds and assists. After an under, it hits 51.3% to 52.2%. Last game's result shifts the next one by less than 0.6 percentage points.

Steals and blocks look like the opposite of the thesis in the raw numbers: unders hit less often after an over. The odds already account for that. Compared against each bet's own price, last game's result adds about nothing in any of the five stats.

Betting it anyway on all five stats, against last game's result at a single book's closing line: 179,629 bets, a 48.9% hit rate against 52.2% needed, and −6.4% on every dollar staked. Shopping the best opening line across every book in the feed only trims the loss to −3.4%.

After a big game the books raise the next line by about 0.10 to 0.12 standard deviations for every SD the player beat his last line by. For points, rebounds and assists the next result then lands right on the new line (slopes −0.002 to +0.009, none statistically different from zero). The thesis needs the books to overreact. They don't. For steals, blocks and 3-pointers made, results carry past the new line, the opposite of what the thesis needs (the odds, not the line, absorb most of it).

In 72 of 72 season-stat pairs across 24 seasons, the opposite of reversal shows up: an above-average game makes the next game more likely to be above average too, by 2.8 percentage points on average for points, rebounds and assists. Roles and minutes carry over from game to game.

Across 16,700 strategy variants, the best on 2022-23 to 2024-25 was “Every under in the regular season · Rebounds · opening line · best line”: +3.6% on 36,037 bets. That is more than luck alone would produce from this many tries (Reality Check p = 0.01). But it depends on shopping every book's line: at a single book the same bet returned −0.8% over the same seasons. On 2025-26, which it never saw, it returned −4.2% on 3,933 bets. 128 strategies pass a 10% false-discovery screen on the earlier seasons, and 125 of them are versions of one bet: shopping every book's opening line, mostly on unders. Together they returned −4.0% on 2025-26, and 14% of them made money.

In 6 of 6 tries, the strategy that had worked best so far lost money the next season.

Unders lose less than overs: −3.4% against −9.6% at a single book's close across the five core stats. That's the familiar lean of prop markets, and it explains why every “bet the under after…” idea looks better than its “bet the over” twin. It still doesn't beat the vig at one book.
